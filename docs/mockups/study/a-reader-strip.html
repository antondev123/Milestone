Notes for the team

Direction A — reader with a now-reading strip

What it optimises for

  • The book as it is. One reflowed page, real headings, objectives box, footnote and page marker. Nothing sits between the learner and the text except a single pinned line.
  • Eyes and ears in sync: word highlight in the page, the same sentence mirrored in the strip, so scrolling away never loses the place. Tap the strip to snap back.
  • Audio off degrades to a plain reader with zero chrome; ask and checkpoint keep working.

What it costs

  • 72px strip plus a floating button on a 390px screen. Long sentences scroll inside the strip like a teleprompter, which some people find busy; a two-line strip would trade height for calm.
  • Word highlight inside Fraunces headings is louder than in body text; we may skip word-level on headings and only rule the sentence.

What the engine needs

  • Word timing from ElevenLabs TTS with_timestamps (character offsets) mapped onto the verbatim leg text in data/courses/pom/verbatim/. Cursor stays block-level on the server; the client owns word and sentence index inside the block.
  • Sentence tap or −1/+1 is a client seek inside the current block (no server call). Crossing a block boundary calls next or goto. A small sentence splitter is needed (abbreviations, quotes, footnote markers glued to the last word).
  • The sheet calls ask with the tapped sentence prepended as context; explain already grounds on the segment. Playback pauses while the sheet is open and resumes at the same word.
  • Checkpoint: MCQ graded locally; open answer goes to the grader (~4 s, mocked here at 1.5 s). Keep the textarea editable on "Partly there" so a retry is one tap.
